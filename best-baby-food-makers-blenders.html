Making your own baby food sounds like a Pinterest fantasy — until you realize it's actually cheaper, healthier, and way easier than you'd expect. The right machine does most of the work. We tested the most popular baby food makers and blenders with real families to find the ones that actually earn their counter space.

What to Look For in a Baby Food Maker

  • Steam + blend combo — one machine that does both saves time and counter space
  • Capacity — batch cooking on Sunday beats making fresh purée at every meal
  • Easy to clean — if it has 12 parts, you'll stop using it by week two
  • Texture control — smooth for beginners, chunky as baby grows
  • BPA-free materials — non-negotiable for anything touching baby's food

Tips for Making Baby Food at Home

  • Batch cook on weekends and freeze in ice cube trays — pop out portions as needed
  • Start with single-ingredient purées (sweet potato, peas, banana) before mixing
  • Add breast milk, formula, or cooking water to thin out thick purées
  • Label everything with the date — frozen purées keep about 3 months
  • Use an app like Kids Meal Planner to track what baby has tried and plan meals for the week
